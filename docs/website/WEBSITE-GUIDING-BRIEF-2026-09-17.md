# Safety Assurance Global Website Guiding Brief

Status: OWNER APPROVED — CONTROLLING PUBLIC-WEBSITE DIRECTION

Date: September 17, 2026

Source basis: `Safety Assurance Global Complete Website Review — September 17, 2026`, combined with owner direction that all normal public-facing company references use the full name **Safety Assurance Global** rather than the abbreviation.

## 1. Commercial north star

The public website must make it easy for a buyer to understand four things without decoding internal assurance terminology:

1. What Safety Assurance Global can be hired to do.
2. Who is expected to perform the work and what verified qualifications support that role.
3. What the customer receives and what evidence supports the work.
4. How to begin an engagement, qualification, teaming, or training conversation.

The strongest positioning is:

> Take a defined safety or quality assignment, staff it appropriately, document the work, and follow problems through to verified correction.

The site should establish this proposition through concrete services, delivery models, people, evidence, sample outputs, and clear next steps.

## 2. Public naming rule

Use **Safety Assurance Global** in normal public-facing copy, headings, service descriptions, calls to action, author bylines, capability language, Government content, Institute content, and public downloads.

Do not use `SAG` as shorthand for the company in normal public presentation.

Internal technical identifiers, repository paths, asset filenames, record IDs, and code variables may retain legacy shorthand where changing them would create technical risk or break provenance.

Formally protected product names may retain their approved names where they are genuine product names rather than shorthand references to the company. Current examples include `SAG Command`, `SAG Academy`, and `SAG SECURE`. This exception must not be used to reintroduce `SAG` as a general company abbreviation.

## 3. Buyer-first language

Lead with the customer's recognizable problem before technical terminology.

Examples:

- Operational readiness: check whether the team, plans, controls, and open actions are ready before work starts.
- Contractor assurance: verify whether contractors are meeting agreed safety, quality, and performance requirements.
- Corrective-action validation: verify that identified problems were actually corrected and supported by closure evidence.
- Embedded HSE support: explain who works with the customer's team, whether delivery is onsite, remote, or mixed, and what day-to-day responsibilities and outputs are included.

Technical terminology may remain, but it should follow a plain-English explanation rather than replace one.

## 4. Every major service must be purchasable

For each core service, make it possible to connect the offer to an actual statement of work. Public pages should explain, where verified and appropriate:

- customer problem or trigger;
- work performed;
- typical delivery model;
- customer inputs;
- typical deliverables;
- decision value or expected use;
- assignment structure;
- scope limitations;
- next step.

Do not publish unsupported staffing, geographic, timing, capacity, outcome, qualification, or availability claims.

## 5. People and evidence before internal governance

Bring credible people and substantiated evidence forward.

Public website priorities:

- concise leadership and technical profiles;
- verified qualifications that are appropriate to publish;
- properly attributed prior experience;
- clear distinction between corporate past performance, individual prior-employer experience, and partner experience;
- permission-cleared project examples when available;
- sample or illustrative deliverables that do not expose customer information;
- accurate procurement identifiers and verification references.

Detailed resumes, credential records, references, performance records, insurance, pricing, sensitive operational information, financial records, and security material should remain in controlled qualification channels unless specifically approved for publication.

## 6. Show the work product

Where supportable, publish sanitized or clearly illustrative examples that show how Safety Assurance Global connects:

requirement → observation/evidence → finding → assigned action → due date → verification → closure status → remaining uncertainty → management reporting.

Examples may include:

- readiness assessment;
- inspection or field-verification summary;
- gap register;
- corrective-action roadmap;
- validation report;
- assurance case or evidence package;
- executive dashboard;
- responsibility matrix;
- service sheet.

Illustrative examples must be labeled as illustrative and must not imply actual customer work.

## 7. Government and major-prime buying paths

Government and major-prime audiences need distinct but connected routes.

Agency and public-sector buyers should be able to identify:

- specific purchasable work;
- deliverables and delivery model;
- verified procurement identifiers/status;
- qualification references;
- government-business contact route;
- supported contracting or teaming paths only where genuinely held or available.

Major-prime buyers should be able to identify:

- why Safety Assurance Global is relevant to a specific pursuit or project need;
- assignment structure and reporting relationships;
- expected deliverables;
- role of Safety Assurance Global relative to the owner, prime, subcontractors, safety leadership, and quality leadership;
- supplier-qualification request path;
- controlled access to supporting qualification evidence.

Do not imply agency endorsement, contract award, contract vehicle, clearance, customer status, or corporate past performance without supporting evidence.

## 8. Capability statement and service sheets

Maintain a dated, downloadable public capability statement once its facts are verified for release.

Create concise service sheets for the highest-priority engagement types so an internal champion, contracting officer, project manager, or prime-contractor representative can circulate the information without having to rewrite the business case.

Any capability statement must preserve the distinction between corporate experience and individual prior-employer experience.

## 9. Training and Institute presentation

Customer-facing Institute and training pages should lead with what can accurately be discussed or arranged now.

For each real offering, publish only verified information such as:

- subject;
- intended audience;
- learning outcomes;
- delivery format;
- duration;
- instructor or reviewer requirements where applicable;
- availability status;
- pilot/custom-development status;
- meaning of completion evidence or credentials.

Do not make internal Course Factory, runtime, registrar, credential-gate, or architecture explanations the primary buying message. Keep governance detail available where it materially supports trust or explains a required boundary.

## 10. Brand-relationship clarity

Introduce Mandavere, the Institute, Academy, SAG Command, or other ecosystem names only where they help the customer make a decision.

When `Powered by Mandavere` appears, explain the current customer-facing relationship accurately. Do not imply required software access, licensing, automated decision authority, or commercial technology availability unless that is actually part of the engagement.

## 11. Independent-assurance role clarity

Where Safety Assurance Global provides independent assurance, embedded support, remediation assistance, or training within the same broader customer relationship, explain assignment-specific role separation accurately.

Clarify, where applicable:

- who commissions the work;
- who receives findings;
- who implements corrective action;
- who validates closure;
- how conflicting roles are separated;
- how reporting and escalation are structured.

Do not imply universal independence across every possible engagement configuration.

## 12. Inquiry and qualification experience

Public inquiry and proposal paths must make it clear whether a submission was actually delivered.

Target experience:

- direct submission where configured and authorized;
- unmistakable success/failure state;
- accurate next-step language;
- no suggestion that an email-preparation fallback equals completed delivery;
- a controlled supplier-qualification request path for records that should not be public.

Do not publish a response-time promise unless Safety Assurance Global can consistently meet it.

## 13. Visual direction

Retain the approved visual system:

- navy, steel/slate, restrained gold;
- realistic maritime and high-consequence operational photography;
- image-forward presentation;
- prominent but controlled Safety Assurance Global seal/identity;
- live HTML for factual copy, qualifications, contact information, procurement identifiers, and claims;
- no fake logos, badges, clients, agency relationships, vessels, employees, or project attribution embedded in representational imagery.

The next visual gains should come increasingly from authentic, permission-cleared Safety Assurance Global evidence rather than repeated generic imagery.

## 14. Public credibility vs. controlled qualification

### Appropriate for the public website when verified

- accurate services and delivery models;
- leadership and technical profiles;
- properly attributed experience examples;
- verified procurement identifiers/status;
- service territory and business contact;
- sample deliverables and reporting approach;
- dated capability statement;
- accurate training availability.

### Appropriate for controlled qualification

- assignment-specific staffing and availability;
- detailed resumes and credential verification;
- references and supporting performance records;
- current registration/certification documents;
- insurance evidence and applicable licenses;
- financial and pricing records;
- security and sensitive operational records.

## 15. Priority order

1. Keep the opening proposition and primary calls to action visible and understandable.
2. Complete and verify reliable inquiry/proposal delivery.
3. Clarify services through plain-English customer situations and defined work products.
4. Strengthen Government and major-prime qualification paths.
5. Publish a verified capability statement and concise service sheets.
6. Add credible people, qualifications, and properly attributed experience.
7. Show sanitized or illustrative sample work and assignment structures.
8. Publish accurate training availability.
9. Reduce repetitive internal-governance language.
10. Explain ecosystem/brand relationships only where they help the buyer.

## 16. Claims and evidence control

No website improvement may invent or imply:

- customers or contracts;
- project participation;
- government or prime-contractor endorsement;
- qualifications or credentials;
- certifications or accreditation;
- staff capacity;
- geographic reach;
- response times;
- outcomes;
- contract vehicles;
- security posture;
- training availability;
- technology availability.

When evidence is missing, use a controlled placeholder, defer publication, or route the subject to private qualification rather than filling the gap with marketing language.

## 17. Acceptance standard

A first-time visitor, government buyer, or major-prime stakeholder should be able to leave the site knowing:

- what Safety Assurance Global can deliver;
- what problem each major service solves;
- what evidence and deliverables they can expect;
- who is responsible for delivery, to the extent publicly verified;
- how relevant experience is attributed;
- how to start an engagement or qualification conversation;
- what remains scope-dependent or privately qualified.

That is the controlling standard for future website changes.
